Privacy notice

Draft, awaiting legal review. The target country for CleverCubs has not been decided, so no compliance with a particular law is claimed. The service is built to a strict common baseline instead. Version 2026-09-draft.

What we collect

  • About the grown-up: name, email address and password (stored only as a secure hash). Mobile number and town are optional.
  • About the child: first name, an optional nickname, date of birth, a username and a chosen animal avatar.
  • Learning activity: which cards were opened, lessons finished, quiz answers and scores, badges and certificates.
  • Feedback you choose to send us.
  • Consent records: which version of these texts you accepted, and when.

What we do not collect

No photos of children, no home address, no school, no gender, no location tracking, no advertising identifiers, and no third-party analytics or advertising. The site loads nothing from other companies' servers.

How the information is used

Only to run CleverCubs for your family: to choose the right level for your child's age, to save and show progress, to give badges, and to answer your feedback. We do not sell or share it.

Who can see it

You can see your own children's information. Your child sees only their own learning space and never your email or other details. CleverCubs administrators can see what they need to run the service, and every administrative change is recorded.

Your choices

  • Download everything we hold about a child as a file, from the child's page.
  • Delete a child's data, or your whole account, at any time.
  • Correct details from the Account page.

Security

Passwords are stored as one-way hashes, sign-ins use secure session cookies, and every page and request checks who is asking. No system is perfectly secure; please tell us straight away if you notice anything unusual.

Areas for legal and privacy review

  • The legal basis and the form of verifiable parental consent for the chosen country.
  • Data retention periods for inactive accounts and deleted data in backups.
  • Hosting location, cross-border transfer and the data processors involved.
  • A data-protection impact assessment for children's data.
  • How requests to access, correct or delete data are verified and answered.
